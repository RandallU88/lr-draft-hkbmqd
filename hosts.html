For Camp Hosts / Landowners

Quiet paid camping, without turning your land into a park.

Liberty books the guests. You host a pad that meets the Liberty standard. You are not becoming a campground brand. Liberty is the brand.

Who we are looking for

Land that wants to stay quiet.

  • Private Ohio land with trees, dirt, and some distance from the house and the road
  • A spot a guest can actually reach
  • High ground, out of the floodplain
  • A landowner who wants quiet guests, not weekend chaos

If the land wants to stay quiet, it fits. If it wants a party field, it does not.

The Liberty site standard

Non-negotiable

  • 1 to 4 pads only
  • Guests bring their own tents and take them home
  • Every pad has an American flag and a Liberty flag on an extendable pole
  • Every pad has a fire ring
  • Every pad has a fire extinguisher
  • Every pad has an emergency radio
  • Quiet rules posted. One party per pad.

Nothing gets listed until the standard is standing.

Structures

Guests bring the tent. You bring the pad.

Summary of the Liberty host structures handout. Check with your local zoning, health, building, and floodplain offices before you build anything with walls, pipes, or a roof.

Do: primitive pad

  • Clear or gravel pad on high ground, floodplain checked
  • Picnic table. Pack-out trash preferred.
  • Fire ring, ABC extinguisher, flags on an unpowered pole, emergency radio
  • Guest-owned tents that leave with the stay
  • A small tool shed for tools only, never for sleeping
  • Keep it quiet. One party per pad.

Don't, unless you have written local OK and Liberty agrees

  • No cabins, tiny homes, or containers for guests to sleep in
  • No yurts or standing glamping or safari tents left up as lodging
  • No plumbing, kitchen, or electric added to a "primitive" shelter
  • No farm building exemptions used for paying overnight guests
  • No more than 4 paid pads on one property
  • No digging or fill in a mapped flood zone without local floodplain sign-off
  • Never market it as a hotel, resort, or glamping
Ask first: a seasonal host tent, a deck, any small sleeping shelter, or any yurt or cabin idea. Talk to Liberty and your local offices before you buy or build.

Verify locally before you build

  • Township or village zoning
  • Local health district
  • Building department
  • Floodplain office
  • Fire district
